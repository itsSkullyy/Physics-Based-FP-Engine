# Physics Based FP Engine

A first person movement and combat platformer built in Unity. The player runs, slides, wall runs, vaults and grapples around a traversal course, fights with a battle axe that can be swung, thrown and recalled, and deals with two kinds of enemies: squads of rifle Grunts and the Stillwalker, a statue that only moves when you aren't looking at it.

Most of the game is built from code at runtime. Particles, hit effects, menus, sounds and enemy placeholders are all generated by scripts, so the project runs without any imported art or audio.

- Unity **6000.0.61f1** (Unity 6), Universal Render Pipeline
- Packages that matter: Input System, Cinemachine 3, AI Navigation (NavMesh), ProBuilder, ML-Agents 4.1.0

## Features

### Movement

`FirstPersonCharacterController.cs`

The controller is one Rigidbody pushed around in `FixedUpdate`. There's no CharacterController and no state machine. Every move is a force or a velocity edit on the same body, which is what lets moves chain into each other without knowing about each other. A slide can go straight into a vault launch, and a wall kick opens a dart window, just because they all read and write the same velocity.

- **Momentum.** Speed builds past the base walk speed the longer you keep moving and decays when you stop. Counter-movement and a separate overspeed drag keep it from feeling floaty without killing speed you earned.
- **Jumping** uses coyote time, a jump buffer, and separate fall and low-jump gravity multipliers, so tapping jump gives a short hop and holding it gives the full height.
- **Slide.** Low friction, a small boost on entry, extra acceleration downhill, and limited steering. It also works in the air. Sliding off a ledge or up a ramp turns into a **slide launch** that converts forward speed into upward speed.
- **Vaulting** checks the obstacle height in front of you and picks a low, regular or jump vault. Each one has its own duration and keeps a different amount of your speed.
- **Wall running** only works on walls tagged `WallRun`. Gravity drops to a quarter, you get pushed into the wall, and you can jump off it. There's a max duration and a cooldown.
- **Wall kicks** off any nearby wall while airborne, capped at two per jump.
- **Darting** is an air dash that only works in a short window after a wall kick or jump. Chaining darts stacks a speed bonus up to five times, and a dart refunds a wall kick so you can keep going up a shaft.
- **Zip into wall run.** Finishing a grapple zip next to a wall-run wall drops you straight into a wall run with a speed boost.

`FirstPersonCameraRig.cs` sits on top of this. It drives a Cinemachine camera as a hard first person mount and adds FOV that widens with speed, strafe/slide/wall-run tilt, a landing dip that only kicks in on falls harder than a normal jump, softened when you land fast or into a slide, and a roll on vaults. The pause menu has a Landing Shake setting (Full / Low / Off) that scales the dip and the landing shake.

### Grapple

`Grappling.cs`

Two modes on one hook:

- **Zip** pulls you straight at the target, accelerating to a capped speed, then flings you up a little and keeps part of your speed when you arrive. It works on every weapon slot. Zip targets can move (enemies implement `IZipTarget`, so the zip tracks them and the enemy decides what happens on arrival).
- **Swing** is a rope you can hang from and reel in or out with the scroll wheel. Only works with the grapple slot equipped.

**Targeting.** Instead of needing a pixel-perfect raycast, it overlap-spheres for every grapple point in range, throws out anything outside a 30 degree cone, and scores the rest on angle and distance. A line of sight check runs against the winner, ignoring the target's own colliders. A homing reticle snaps onto the current target.

**Rope solver.** The rope is a one-sided distance constraint solved on velocity, not a spring or a joint. Every physics step it predicts next frame's velocity (including gravity), removes any part of it that would stretch the rope, and adds a correction proportional to how far past the rope length you already are, capped so it can't explode. That keeps swings stiff without jitter. On top of that there's:

- automatic slack take-up, so the rope never goes loose and then snaps tight
- different gravity, stiffness and damping when you're above the anchor, so swinging over the top feels controlled instead of collapsing
- ground swinging, where the rope can pull you along a slide without lifting you off the floor
- jump-off and release boosts

The rope is drawn as a sagging LineRenderer that shoots out from the gun tip.

### Battle axe

`BattleAxe.cs`, `ThrownAxe.cs`, `WeaponSlots.cs`

The axe is the main weapon and also a movement tool.

- **Swing.** Short windup, swing and recover phases with a hit window. The hit is a sphere cast from the camera.
- **Mace bounce.** Hitting a surface bounces you off it, mixing the surface normal with your current velocity. Hitting the ground while falling turns your fall speed into upward speed, so you can pogo off things. A bounce also releases the grapple.
- **Glass smash.** Hitting a breakable wall skips the normal bounce and launches you forward through the hole.
- **Throw.** Hold primary to charge (with a meter and a pulled-back pose) or press secondary to throw instantly. The thrown axe inherits half your velocity.
- **Recall and pickup.** Walk over a loose axe to grab it, press G to call it back from anywhere, or zip at a loose axe to pull it to you. A cooldown ring shows when recall is ready.

**How the thrown axe hits things.** A spinning axe at 42 m/s with a normal collider would tunnel through thin walls. `ThrownAxe` turns its colliders off and simulates its own arc. Each physics step it splits the movement into sub-steps (no longer than 0.22 m each) and sphere-casts a continuous chain of segments along the path of the axe head, plus a second chain along the handle's pivot. Because the head path follows the spin, the blade can't skip past a surface between frames. When it hits something it embeds at a set depth, blends its angle toward the surface normal, levels its roll and parents itself to the surface. A stuck axe becomes a grapple point.

`WeaponSlots` handles the three slots (axe, grapple, empty hands). Throwing the axe swaps you onto the grapple and catching it puts you back on the axe.

### Breakable walls

`BreakableWall.cs`, `WallShard.cs`, `RunThroughProbe.cs`

Walls that break from an axe swing, a thrown axe sticking into them, or running into them fast enough. The wall's mesh is cut into a grid of shards (default 4x4x2, capped at 40 live shards) with some random size jitter so it doesn't look like a perfect grid. Each shard is a rigidbody with a convex mesh collider, and the shards get a burst force, a push in the direction of the hit and some spin.

Running through is detected by a trigger the wall spawns just in front of itself (`RunThroughProbe`). It checks on stay as well as enter, so if you dart or slide-launch while already inside the trigger it still counts. Shards stop costing anything once they settle and shrink away after a few seconds. Walls fire `Shattered` and `Respawned` events, which the AI uses as noises.

### Game feel

`JuiceFX.cs`, `ImpactFrames.cs`, `CameraShaker.cs`, `PlayerJuice.cs`

Every hit, landing and pickup goes through the same three things:

- **JuiceFX** builds all particles at runtime as actual 3D meshes: low-poly spheres and shards. Cel shading is baked into per-face UVs against a tiny two-colour palette texture, and outlines are inverted hulls drawn flat black behind the mesh. Since the materials are opaque and can't fade, particles shrink out instead. It has dust, sparks, impact bursts, landing dust and air puffs.
- **ImpactFrames** does an ULTRAKILL style impact frame. It sets `timeScale` to 0 for a moment, grabs the screen and recolours it into a hard red and black two-tone, and lays speed lines and a chromatic split over it. The physics timestep scales with the freeze so nothing tunnels through on resume, and there's a hard cap on freeze length so spamming hits can't lock the game.
- **CameraShaker** uses the trauma model (shake = trauma squared, decaying over time) plus spring-driven directional kicks and FOV punches. It doesn't move the camera itself. It exposes offsets that the camera rig adds in, so the two never fight. It runs on unscaled time so it keeps shaking during hitstop.

`PlayerJuice` watches the controller's public state and fires these off for landings, footsteps, slides, wall runs, wall kicks and darts, without the controller needing to know it exists.

### Audio

`Audio/Synth.cs`, `Audio/GameSounds.cs`, `Audio/PlayerSounds.cs`, `AI/Core/EnemySounds.cs`

There are no audio files in the project. `Synth` generates every sound from code: oscillators that keep their phase for smooth pitch sweeps, noise, one-pole filters and soft clipping. Each recipe is seeded so it sounds the same every run, and every clip is RMS-normalised to the loudness the recipe asks for, so nothing has to be balanced by hand. Clips are cached and played through pooled 2D and 3D AudioSources.

`PlayerSounds` attaches itself to the player on scene load and plays footsteps, slides, wind, wall runs, the grapple, axe swings/throws/hits, damage and course sounds based on the same state `PlayerJuice` reads. Enemies get their own set: gunshots, bullet whizzes, Grunt footsteps, radio blips, alert stings, grenade beeps, Stillwalker steps and cracks. Any of these can be replaced by dragging a real clip onto the enemy's sound fields.

### Course, timer and menus

`CourseTimer.cs`, `CourseHUD.cs`, `LevelGoal.cs`, `LevelCompleteMenu.cs`, `PauseMenu.cs`, `StartZone.cs`, `FinishZone.cs`, `Deathplane.cs`

- The course clock starts as soon as the scene loads and shows at the top of the screen as `M:SS:MMM`. It runs on unscaled time on purpose, so hitstop and impact frames don't give you free time. Pausing sets a separate `Paused` flag to stop it.
- The **level goal** is a floating, spinning pickup at the end of the course. Touching it stops the clock and grades your time against S/A/B/C/D thresholds (anything slower is an F). These are set per level in the inspector.
- Finishing doesn't pause anything. A **results panel** slides in on the right while you keep moving: your time, your best (or "New Best!", saved in PlayerPrefs), and the rank letter stamping down in its colour. Then the level's **exit portal** tears open and a marker on screen points at it. Going through takes you back to the tutorial, and the panel slides away a few seconds later.
- **Pause menu** with Resume, Restart and Close Game. Restart reloads whichever scene you're standing in (the level or the tutorial).
- `StartZone` / `FinishZone` are an older manual start gate and finish line. They still work but aren't needed now that the timer starts by itself.
- `Deathplane` kills or damages the player and sends them back to a respawn point, going through `PlayerHealth` so death only ever happens one way.

### Input and UI

`PlayerInputRouter.cs`, `GameAction.cs`, `InputRebinding.cs`, `RebindMenu.cs`, `GamepadMenu.cs`, `PlayerHUD.cs`, `PlayerHealth.cs`

- `GameAction` wraps a Unity `InputAction` so gameplay code can keep writing `input.jump.Pressed` or `input.slide.Held` while still getting gamepad support, composite bindings and rebinding.
- `PlayerInputRouter` finds its actions with reflection. Adding a new action is one public `GameAction` field plus a default binding, and it's automatically enabled, saved, loaded and listed in the rebind menu.
- `InputRebinding` handles listening for a new key, keeps keyboard and gamepad rebinds separate so a stick twitch can't be bound by accident, and reports conflicts.
- All UI is IMGUI (`OnGUI`). There's no Canvas, UGUI or TextMeshPro anywhere, so nothing has to be imported or set up for the HUD and menus to work. `GamepadMenu` adds d-pad and arrow key navigation to the IMGUI menus, and only acts once per frame since `OnGUI` runs several times a frame.
- The HUD has a health bar, a speed bar and the three weapon slot icons (generated if no sprites are assigned). Health regenerates after five seconds without damage.

## Enemy AI

`Assets/Scripts/AI`

Enemies are built on a small set of AI systems written for this project, all in `AI/Core`. Each enemy uses a mix of them.

### Core systems

| System | File | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchical state machine | `HierarchicalStateMachine.cs` | Every enemy's top-level brain. States can have child states, and a parent's transitions are checked before its children's, so something like "got staggered" only has to be written once on the parent. |
| Behaviour trees | `BehaviourTree.cs` | Built in code (sequences, selectors, decorators, leaves). `OnStop` always runs when a node finishes, fails or gets aborted, so a leaf can always give back things it reserved like a shooting token or a cover spot. Trees can watch a blackboard key and restart when it changes. |
| Blackboards | `Blackboard.cs` | Key/value memory that chains: follower → squad → global. Reads fall through to the parent board. Every key has a version number so trees can tell when a value changed. |
| Utility AI | `UtilityBrain.cs` | Infinite axis utility. Each action has considerations that go through response curves and get multiplied together, with Dave Mark's compensation factor so actions with more considerations aren't unfairly scored lower. |
| GOAP | `GoapPlanner.cs` | World state is a set of true/false facts packed into a 64-bit mask. A* searches over actions until the goal's facts are true, with a heuristic that never overestimates so it finds the cheapest plan. Each planned action runs as its own behaviour tree. |
| EQS | `EnvironmentQuery.cs` | Environment queries in the style of Unreal's EQS. Generate candidate points, run weighted tests on each, and pick the best. A negative score throws a point out completely. Queries are spread across frames. |
| Tactical map | `TacticalMap.cs` | When a level loads it samples points across the NavMesh over a few frames and records low and high cover in 8 directions for each, how exposed the spot is, plus every grapple point and wall-run wall. Squads plan using this instead of raycasting everything live. |
| AI director | `AIDirector.cs` | Shared state for all enemies: the global blackboard, noises, squads, shooting tokens so only a few enemies fire at once, claimed positions so two enemies don't pick the same spot, bodies, the thrown axe, and the F3/F4 debug overlays. Spawns itself when the first enemy needs it. |
| Player tracker | `PlayerMotionTracker.cs` | Watches the player for the AI: predicted position, where a jump will land, how predictable the movement is, landings and noises. F8 records runs to CSV, which the training scene replays as a fake player. |

`EnemyAgent` is the base class: health, hits from the axe (melee, thrown, deflected bullet, zip slide), sight and hearing checks, NavMesh movement, animator helpers, ragdoll on death and reset. It also catches enemies getting stuck: if one makes no progress along its path for 1.5 seconds it snaps back onto the NavMesh and re-paths, and if it's still stuck the move fails so the behaviour tree and utility AI pick something else. A path that can't reach its target counts as failed too, instead of the enemy standing at the end of it. The F3 overlay shows STUCK or how many times it got unstuck.

### Grunts

`AI/Grunt`

Rifle soldiers who work in squads of up to six: a leader and followers split into two fireteams.

**Leader** (3 hits). Sees in a 220 degree cone out to 60 m with peripheral vision past that, and hears noises the player makes. The top-level states are Idle, Suspicious, Alert, Combat, Staggered and Dead. Combat has its own sub-states for engaging, diving, throwing grenades and countering. While engaging, utility AI picks an intent (hold and shoot, reposition, flank, push, evade, take cover, grenade), and each intent is a behaviour tree. EQS finds firing positions, flank spots and cover, with tests for spreading out from teammates, staying perpendicular to the player and being able to peek.

**Squad tactics** (`SquadTactics.cs`) is the squad's brain and runs on GOAP. Its facts come from what the squad actually knows plus the tactical map, not from the player's real position, so the squad can be wrong about where you are. Goals are Find, Protect, Trap, Flush and Pressure, and the actions it plans with are search sweeps, bounding advances, suppression lines, fireteam flanks, pincers, grenade flushes, pushing cover, cutting off escape routes and rallying. It also handles idle formations (wedge, line, column, ring) and snaps formation slots onto cover.

**Followers** (1 hit) run behaviour trees driven by the orders the leader writes to the squad blackboard. Their aim lags behind you and gets worse the more of them are shooting together. A follower on its own will patrol and search by itself. Once a follower knows the leader is dead, it panics for good.

**Stealth layer.**
- Each Grunt has an awareness meter that builds faster the closer you are, with **?** and **!** markers above its head. Seeing you fills the meter. Hearing you only makes them suspicious.
- Squads move through Calm, Suspicious, Searching and Combat. Patrol loops are generated automatically if none are placed.
- When a follower notices something, it radios it in. The leader either sends someone to check it out or goes himself while the others watch.
- Kills nobody saw stay unknown until someone finds the body. The squad only falls apart from a dead leader once someone actually sees the body, and followers will go check on a leader who's gone quiet.
- The thrown axe works as a lure. It makes a 38 m noise and the squad walks over to it as a group. The cost is that recalling it is visible and audible, and the squad will follow its path back to you.

**Combat details.**
- Bullets are slow tracers you can dodge, and an axe swing deflects them. They sweep a sphere every frame instead of using a collider, so they can't tunnel.
- Leaders throw grenades: a red cube that beeps faster until it explodes. The fuse is 4 seconds, long enough to run up and hit it with the axe, which sends it flying at the nearest Grunt. The swing bats any live grenade within 45 degrees of where you're aiming, so you don't have to hit the cube exactly. A leader won't throw at his own feet or while you're rushing him, and any Grunt near a live grenade runs from it after a short, random reaction time (slow ones still get caught). A grenade you batted back can't be dodged. The explosion (`ExplosionFX`) is built at runtime the same way as JuiceFX, with a flash, fireball, smoke, debris, a shockwave ring and "BOOM" text.
- Zipping into a standing Grunt bounces you up off him and staggers him. Zipping in while sliding hits him and carries you through without losing speed.
- Zipping is easy to see coming, so they get good at shooting it. When you grapple a Grunt he and up to two squadmates who can see you flash "!", wait 0.15 to 0.3 s, then snap-fire short tight bursts aimed ahead along your zip line. The kill is still one hit, it just costs health now. Swinging the axe still deflects the bullets.

### Stillwalker

`AI/Stillwalker`

A stone figure that freezes as soon as you look at it. It counts as "seen" when it's within 35 degrees of your view, closer than 70 m, and there's nothing blocking the line between your camera and its chest.

- It wakes when you come within 25 m, when it sees you, or when it hears you. After that it always knows where you are and measures distance along the NavMesh, so it walks around walls to follow you.
- While unseen it stalks you. It starts out about 12 m away and creeps closer the longer you go without looking at it, down to 5 m after 8 seconds. Spotting it knocks that build-up back by half. It moves faster while you're facing away or it's out of your sight, and if it falls far behind it runs to catch up.
- When you're moving it looks a couple of seconds ahead along your route for a spot that's out of your sight, looks onto where you'll be, and that it can reach first (EQS AmbushPos). It runs there, goes still with its eyes dimmed, and jumps you when you come within 12 m.
- If it creeps within 7 m without being seen it pounces, whether you've landed or not. The first time it gets within 9 m behind you there's a stone scrape, so a jump from behind is never silent.
- When you land on the ground within 20 m, it winds up for 0.2 seconds and launches at where it predicts you'll be. A ring marks the landing spot. You can dodge it, but it's hard. The jump is swept against the level before it leaves, so it stops short of walls and flattens out under low ceilings instead of flying through them.
- Swinging the axe does nothing to it. It only cracks from a falling pogo hit or a thrown axe. Four cracks shatter it, and cracks heal if you leave it alone for 3 seconds.
- Looking at it stops it walking, not reacting. A crack makes it jerk away from the hit even while you watch, then it retreats until the cracks heal. The more cracked it is, the further and faster it runs (24 / 32 / 40 m). It backs away facing you, steered by the ML policy or, without one, an EQS query that looks for a spot out of your sight. It only launches while retreating if you land within 6 m.
- One hit from death it bolts: 18 m/s, keeps running even while you look at it, and never launches.
- Glowing red cracks show how close it is to breaking. Each crack grows a jagged line from where it was hit. More cracks means longer lines, a brighter glow and a faster pulse, and one hit from death it splits all over and flickers. The cracks fade when it heals.
- Once it's cracked it can sidestep a pogo coming down on it. The chance goes up with each crack, with a cooldown after each dodge, so the thrown axe becomes the reliable way to finish it.
- You can zip at it, but it sidesteps at the last moment and swats you as you fly past (45 damage and a big knockback). If it has no room to dodge you hit it face first and get swatted anyway. The exception is the second after a launch when it's stuck: zip into it then and you bounce straight up over it, ready to pogo.
- States: Dormant / Hunting (Stalking: Moving or Watched, Retreating: Fleeing or Frozen, Dodging, Launching: Windup or Flight, Recovering) / Shattered. Where it moves while stalking or retreating is decided by a trained ML policy if one is assigned, otherwise by utility AI (CloseIn, CatchUp, Ambush, Lurk, HoldBack) and the EQS flee query. The policy only does the early part of stalking: it steers when it can see you within 22 m on a short path, and hands over to the rules once it has been unseen long enough to want to be closer than 10 m (it was trained to hold about 12 m). Behind walls, while ambushing, or if it stands still for 0.6 s while you can't see it, the rules drive too. The F6 menu shows its state, who's steering and why, the unseen build-up and ambush status, and has buttons to wake it, max out the creep-in, set up an ambush where you're looking, force an intent, or play the creep sound. Dodging, flinching and launching are always hand-written.

### ML-Agents (Stillwalker)

`StillwalkerAgent.cs`, `StillwalkerHunterBot.cs`, `Training/stillwalker.yaml`, `Scenes/Stillwalker Training.unity`

The ML policy decides where the Stillwalker goes (a direction and whether to hurry), both while it stalks you and while it runs away hurt. All the actual rules (freezing when seen, launching, cracking, dodging, bolting at one hit from death) stay hand-written in `Stillwalker.cs`. That keeps it fair and stops the training from finding exploits.

- **Observations (51):**
  - Where the player is, where they're predicted to be and how they're moving, all in the Stillwalker's local space.
  - Their height, vertical speed and time grounded or airborne, plus whether they're swinging, zipping, wall running or sliding.
  - Whether it's being looked at, how far off-centre it is on screen, and whether it's out in the open at all.
  - Threats: a zip aimed at it, or a thrown axe about to hit.
  - Its own state: cracks, whether it's one hit from death, how close the cracks are to healing, launch ready, its own velocity.
  - Escape probes: in 8 directions around it, how much room it has to run and whether 6 m that way is hidden from you.
  - A player profile: running averages of how fast you move, how much you're airborne, how much you watch it, whether you close in or keep your distance, and how often you come down on top of it.
- **Memory:** the network has an LSTM (about 6 seconds of decisions). Together with the profile, that's what lets it pick up a player's habits instead of only reacting to the current frame.
- **Actions:** a continuous move direction plus a discrete "go fast" choice. Small moves count as standing still.
- **Rewards:**
  - Attacking: rewards when the player lands in launch range and when a launch or contact hit connects. Penalties when the player lands out of range and for missed launches.
  - Staying alive: a small penalty every step it's being watched, penalties for cracks, and a big one for being shattered (which ends the episode).
  - Escaping: while hurt, a reward for being far away and out of sight and a penalty for being within 8 m, both bigger at one hit from death. A reward when its cracks heal, and when a thrown axe misses. The heal reward is kept small enough that getting hit on purpose never pays.
- **Training data:** episodes alternate between two fake players.
  - F8 recordings of real runs, which show how players actually move.
  - `StillwalkerHunterBot`, which hunts it down on the NavMesh, jumps to pogo it and bounces off. Each episode it picks a style (Diver, Circler, Kiter) and a skill level.
  - Both throw simulated axes that lead the target and miss if it moved or got behind cover. Some episodes start it already cracked so it gets plenty of practice running.
- **Curriculum:** the trainer config ramps the hunter's skill from clumsy to good, and starts with more pre-cracked episodes.
- **Training:** PPO, 3x256 network, normalised inputs, LSTM memory, set to 6M steps. The `stillwalker02` run was stopped at about 2.74M steps. Its checkpoints, TensorBoard logs and config are in `results/stillwalker02`, and the model the game uses is `Assets/Enemies/Stillwalker/Stillwalker.onnx`.

The old `stillwalker01` model (15 observations) won't load with this version. If a model doesn't match, the agent logs a warning, switches itself off, and the Stillwalker uses the hand-written rules until a retrained model is assigned. The same happens with no model at all, or without the ML-Agents package.

```bash
mlagents-learn Assets/Scripts/AI/Stillwalker/Training/stillwalker.yaml --run-id=stillwalker02
```

Then press Play in `Stillwalker Training.unity`. More F8 recordings (different styles: careful, aggressive, lots of grappling) give it more players to learn from.

## Portals, room builder and tutorial

Built on `Experimental-Changes`, merged into `main` along with the enemy AI.

### Portals

`Portal.cs`, `PortalManager.cs`, `PortalCompositeFeature.cs`, `PortalSceneEnvironment.cs`, `Shaders/PortalComposite.shader`

Seamless portals between different scenes. You can run, slide or dart through at full speed with no loading screen, and you can grapple and throw the axe through them too.

- **Scene streaming.** `PortalManager` loads the linked scene additively before you reach the portal. It counts references and waits a little before unloading, so walking back and forth near a portal doesn't keep loading and unloading the scene. It also makes sure only one player and one camera exist, since every scene has its own player rig.
- **Linking.** Two portals find each other by scene name and portal ID through a registry, once both scenes are loaded.
- **Rendering.** Each portal has a camera that renders the other side to a render texture, using an oblique near clip plane so geometry behind the exit portal can't block the view. Instead of drawing that texture on a quad, a custom URP renderer feature draws it straight into the main camera's buffer, stencil-clipped to the portal's shape. It runs after opaques, so real objects in front still block it, and before post-processing, so both sides get the same colour grading in one pass. Portals seen through other portals aren't drawn again (no recursion).
- **Crossing.** The teleport is checked in physics, but also against the camera every rendered frame. The camera is interpolated between physics steps, so at high speed the view could pass through the portal a frame before the body does, and you'd see a frame of the room you just left. A small lockout stops both checks from firing on the same crossing.
- **Teleporting.** `Portal` has a transform that maps any position, rotation or velocity from one side to the other. The player gets moved with `FirstPersonCharacterController.TeleportTo`, which keeps the look direction correct and tells Cinemachine it was a teleport so the camera doesn't slide across. The thrown axe and grapple aim ray check their paths against portals too, since neither of them uses trigger colliders.
- The portal surface is a thin box with a frame instead of a flat quad, so it still looks like it has depth from an angle and the near plane doesn't clip it while you're halfway through.
- **Closed portals.** Tick `Start Closed` and a portal stays invisible and can't be crossed until something calls `Open()`. It then drops onto the floor under it, a slit of light forms top to bottom, and it tears open sideways with an overshoot, a burst of particles, a camera shake, a light flash and a synthesised whoosh. Opening one side opens its partner too. The level's exit portal and the arrival spot in the tutorial work this way.
- **Enemies go through too.** Each open portal adds a NavMesh link from the floor in front of it to the floor in front of its partner, so an enemy's path to you runs through the portal like any other route. The moment an enemy steps on the link it's carried out the other side and keeps going. When you go through, enemies that saw you in the last 10 seconds (or heard you near the portal), and squads that knew where you were, get the far side as your last known spot. The Stillwalker measures distance through portals as well, so it keeps stalking at the right range instead of thinking you're a scene away.
- **NavMesh on the other side.** A scene with portals but no baked NavMesh (the tutorial, right now) gets one built from its colliders when it loads. Baking a `NavMeshSurface` there skips this.
- **One player.** A streamed-in scene's own Player copy is awake for a frame before it gets switched off. `PortalManager.FindPlayer()` always returns the live one, and the camera shaker and input router won't let the copy take over. Restarting goes through `PortalManager.ReloadScene` so the carried-over player is cleared first.

### Room builder

`Assets/Editor/RoomBuilder.cs`

Editor tool at **Tools > ProBuilder Rooms > Convert Selection To Room**. It takes any closed ProBuilder shape (box, L-shape, corridor) and turns it into a room you can stand inside. It flips the faces inward, puts it on the Ground layer so the controller and grapple can actually detect it (ProBuilder uses Default, which the ground check ignores), uses a non-convex collider so you aren't sealed inside a solid block, and fixes shadow settings so light doesn't come through the backfaces. It's a single undo step.

### Tutorial level

A first draft of the tutorial level in `Mirror Grapple Scene` (rebuilt with the room builder). The flow is tutorial, then through the portal into `Grapple Scene`, then out through the exit portal that opens at the end, which brings you back to the tutorial at the `Arrival Portal`. Drag the `Exit Portal` (Grapple Scene) and `Arrival Portal` (tutorial) to wherever they should appear; `LevelGoal.exitPortal` points at the exit. There's also a `FrustumErrorProbe.cs` debug script for portal rendering sitting in a stash on `Experimental-Changes`.

## Level setup notes

- Ground: layer 6 `Ground`. The controller's ground check only sees this layer.
- Grapple points: layer 7 `Grapple Mask` with tag `Grapple`.
- Wall-run walls: layer 8 with tag `WallRun`.
- Enemies need a baked NavMesh. The tactical map builds itself from it.
- HUD and menu components (`PlayerHUD`, `RebindMenu`, `CourseHUD`, `PauseMenu`) go on the `Player` object. Services like `CourseTimer`, `JuiceFX`, `ImpactFrames` and `AIDirector` create themselves when first needed.
- Enemy art is optional. Grunts and the Stillwalker run as placeholder shapes until models are added. `Assets/Enemies/README.md` lists what to download from Mixamo and how to hook it up.
